ESC Programming Guide

How to set up the Hobbywing QuicRun WP 10BL120 G2 ESC that comes in the Motor / ESC Combo, using the Hobbywing LED Program Card from the shopping list. This isn't in CURV LAB's build guide at all — it's Hobbywing's own setup process, condensed from their official manual into one page.

Sourced from Hobbywing's own manual for the QuicRun 10BL60 & 10BL120 G2 (Sensored), not from CURV LAB. We read it end to end and pulled out just what applies to this build.
Official manual (PDF)
⚠ Adult-led, wheels off the ground. Hobbywing's manual is direct about this: always keep the wheels in the air when the ESC is powered on for calibration or programming — a full-power motor with nothing to grip isn't something a first-time driver should be holding. (The manual also suggests removing the pinion gear first if you can; by this point in the build it's already meshed and set-screwed in from Session 7, so "wheels off the ground, on a stand" is the mitigation this guide actually relies on.) Also: connect the battery only with the ESC switched off, and match + to + and − to − exactly — a reversed connection destroys the ESC and isn't covered under warranty.

1. Throttle range calibration

Do this first, with the transmitter — not the program card. It teaches the ESC where "neutral," "full forward," and "full reverse" actually are on your specific transmitter. Redo it any time the ESC or transmitter changes, or if you adjust the throttle trim.

1

Turn on the transmitter first. If it has D/R (Dual Rate), EPA (End Point Adjustment), or ATL (Adjustable Travel Limit) settings on the throttle channel, set them to 100%, and throttle trim to 0 — skip this if your transmitter is still at factory defaults. These live in your transmitter's own menu system, not on the ESC; check its manual if you can't find them.

2

With the ESC switched off, hold the SET button and switch the ESC on. The red LED starts flashing (the motor beeps too) — release SET right away. If you don't release it within 3 seconds, the ESC drops into programming mode instead and you'll need to restart from this step.

3

Leave the throttle trigger at neutral and press SET. The green LED flashes once (one beep) — neutral is stored.

4

Pull the trigger to full forward and press SET. Green flashes twice (two beeps) — the forward end point is stored.

5

Push the trigger to full reverse/brake and press SET. Green flashes three times (three beeps) — the reverse end point is stored, and the motor is ready to drive normally.

2. Programming with the LED Program Card

This is the card from the shopping list — it replaces the tedious "count the LED blinks" method with buttons and a readout. One card programs both ESCs; it's not a per-car item.

1

With the ESC switched off, connect the ESC's 3-pin throttle/programming cable to the program card's port — match the − and + markings on both ends, they only make sense one way.

2

Power on the ESC. After a couple seconds the card shows the current value of every parameter.

3

Press ITEM to step through the 12 parameters below, VALUE to change the selected one, then OK to save it.

The 12 parameters

ParameterFactory defaultWhat it does
1. Running ModeForward/Reverse with BrakeHobbywing's own manual calls this the "training" mode — the car only reverses after it's come to a full stop first, so a stuck or confused throttle input can't suddenly throw it into reverse. Leave it here for a first driver.
2. Cutoff Voltage3.0V/cellCuts power once the battery gets too low to protect the LiPo. On the 3S Zeee battery that's a 9.0V total cutoff — a sensible default, no reason to change it.
3. PunchLevel 7How aggressively the motor responds to throttle input. Higher = snappier off the line. Leave at default until you know you want softer starts.
4. Drag Brake Force0%How much the car brakes on its own when you let off the throttle, like engine braking. 0% means it coasts freely.
5. Max Brake Force75%How hard the car brakes at full brake-trigger. The manual warns a very high setting can shorten braking time but stress the pinion/spur gears.
6. Max Reverse Force25%Reverse speed. Hobbywing recommends a smaller value here to avoid mistakes from reversing too quickly — a good one to leave alone for a beginner.
7. Neutral Range9%How wide the "dead zone" around neutral is. Only worth raising if the car creeps forward/back on its own at rest.
8. Boost Timing4°Motor timing that scales with throttle input. Affects top-end power. Leave at default — this isn't a race car yet.
9. Turbo Timing0°Extra timing at full throttle for more top speed. The manual's own caution: it raises current and heat in both the motor and ESC. Leave off (0°) for now.
10. Turbo Delay0.2sHow long you have to hold full throttle before Turbo Timing kicks in. Irrelevant while Turbo Timing is off.
11. BEC Voltage6.0VPower sent to the servo and receiver. We checked — the Miuzei 25KG servo in this build is rated 4.8–8.4V, so either 6.0V or 7.4V is safe on it, but there's no reason to change the default. Never set this above your servo's rated voltage if you swap servos later.
12. Motor RotationCCWWhich way the motor spins. Only touch this if the car drives backward from what the throttle trigger says — then flip it to CW instead of re-wiring anything.

3. Factory reset

If programming goes sideways, this puts every parameter above back to the defaults in the table.

With the LED program card connected, press RESET, then press OK to save. Power the ESC off and back on to confirm the defaults took.
