Session 1 — Press the Chassis Plate Inserts

⏱ ~30 min 👤 Kid-led, adult supervises the press 🛠 Insert press (not the soldering iron)
What we changed: the official guide presses these same 10 inserts in two separate moments — step 1.01 (into the two Chassis Plate Posts) and step 1.2 (into the main plate, several pages later in the flow). Since we're using a press instead of a soldering iron, there's no reason to split them — we do all 10 in one sitting, before any fastening starts.

What you need

Chassis Plate
qty 1 · printed, Set A
Chassis Plate Post
qty 2 · printed, Set A
M3 × 5.7mm threaded insert
qty 10 · brass
Insert press + M3 tip
your tool, not the book's

Steps

1

Find your parts: the Chassis Plate (the big long one), the 2 Chassis Plate Posts (small tubes with holes through the middle), and count out 10 brass inserts. Lay them out so nothing rolls away.

2

Get a grown-up to help set up the insert press with the M3 tip. This is the "adult part" of the job — presses can pinch fingers, so hands stay clear while it's running.

3

Press 1 insert into each Chassis Plate Post — 2 posts, 2 inserts. Keep the insert straight as it goes in, not tilted.

4

Press the remaining 8 inserts into the main Chassis Plate, at the holes marked in the guide's diagram below.

5

Check every insert: it should sit flush and straight, flat with the plastic, not leaning to one side. If one looks crooked, tell a grown-up — it's easier to back it out and redo it now than after the plate is full of other parts.

6

Put the finished Chassis Plate and both posts into a labeled bag — "Session 2: fasten posts + start center diff" — so they're easy to find next time.

Why we skip the soldering iron: the book's method heats a soldering iron to 250–350°F and melts each insert in by hand — hot, slow, and not something a 10-year-old should hold. A press does the same job with squarer, more consistent inserts and no hot tip near little hands. Anywhere the guide calls for the soldering iron and insert tips, use the press instead.
